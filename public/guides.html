PRACTICAL LEARNING

Security guides for small teams

A starting point for better decisions. These guides are general educational material, not incident-specific professional advice.

GUIDE 01

Prioritize vulnerabilities by exposure and exploitation

Not every vulnerability is equally urgent. Start with software you actually operate, then determine whether a vulnerable version is present and reachable. An entry in the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is evidence that exploitation has been observed in the wild, so it is a practical input into prioritization rather than merely a theoretical score.

Build a simple triage record that includes the asset owner, version, network accessibility, vulnerability reference, compensating controls, remediation owner, and verification date. Compare available vendor remediation guidance to the CISA required action; don't automatically assume a patch is suitable for your environment.

Federal agencies have specific CISA deadlines. Private businesses can use the dates as useful signals, but should set their own risk-based commitments. Test mitigations in an appropriate environment and track exceptions. Primary source: CISA KEV catalog.

GUIDE 02

Roll out multifactor authentication without causing chaos

Start with privileged accounts, email, VPN, and critical business systems. Identify any service accounts or legacy applications that cannot support MFA before enforcing policies broadly. Prefer phishing-resistant MFA when your platforms support it.

Prepare recovery procedures before deployment: backup factors, help-desk identity verification, and a clear process for lost devices. Test with a small group, monitor lockouts, then expand. MFA reduces account compromise risks but is not a substitute for patching, device protection, and good access management.

Primary sources: CISA MFA guidance and NIST digital identity guidance.

GUIDE 03

The first hour after a suspected security incident

Keep people safe, assess what is happening, preserve evidence, and activate your organization's incident response procedures. Record the time and source of each observation. Isolate affected systems when appropriate, but consider operational consequences and evidence preservation before altering or powering them off.

Notify the responsible incident commander, IT/security leadership, and legal or compliance teams as required by your organization. Don't promise that a system is clean until there is evidence. Maintain a single decision log covering hypotheses, actions, and owners.

Reporting duties differ by sector and jurisdiction. Your actual plan and applicable law govern. Primary source: CISA incident response plan basics.
